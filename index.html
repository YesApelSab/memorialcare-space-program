01 — Summary of Scope & Current State

Opportunity Understanding

MemorialCare stands at a decision point — and the organizations that navigate these moments best do so with data, not instinct. CBRE's expanded scope delivers exactly that: validated headcount, a refreshed space program, and rigorous building-by-building analysis. Where gaps exist, we developed test fits and real cost implications in partnership with Turner & Townsend. The result is a set of defensible, numbers-backed scenarios that tell MemorialCare exactly what it would cost to stay — and puts them in the driver's seat with any potential buyer.

01

Work Patterns Are Function-Driven, Not Policy-Driven

Every leader described attendance behaviors governed by the nature of the work, not an enterprise hybrid policy. Call-center navigators use a fixed 20% onsite / 80% remote split. AP and Payroll anchor to Tuesdays for check-printing and payroll run schedules. IS is almost entirely remote, coming in only for project-based collaboration. No single seat-sharing ratio or flex policy is applicable campus-wide.

02

Privacy and Confidentiality Are Operational Requirements

Multiple departments have genuine privacy needs that constrain space design. AP has locked check-printing equipment in a badged suite. Payroll requires an isolated badged suite. HR employee relations conversations are audible from the front entrance. Legal requires complete isolation with its own badge system. PFS handles PHI at the desk level. These are compliance and HR risk requirements — not amenity preferences.

03

Purpose-Built Specialty Spaces Must Be Preserved

The Navigation Center's Red Building space was designed to call-center standards — electronic sit-stand desks, under-the-tree training area, treadmill workstations, locker banks. VP Akhil Tayal called it "by far one of the best spaces" across 100+ call centers on four continents and explicitly framed it as a hiring motivator. MMF's floor runs a desk reservation system and has a more modern, open interior. Both leaders made retention of their spaces a condition of any consolidation.

04

Collaboration Space Is the Scarce Resource, Not Desk Count

Across nearly every interview, the complaint was too few meeting and collaboration rooms — not too few desks. IS's entire remote population comes in for project-based work; project rooms matter more than hoteling seats. HR runs new hire orientations for up to 700 attendees and currently uses a parking lot for all-staff BBQs. The campus-wide conference room shortage is a persistent, unresolved problem.

05

Headcount and Growth Are Genuinely Uncertain

Several leaders could not provide firm future headcount because strategic decisions remain open. Navigation Center grew from 110 to 150 in seven months with no clear ceiling. PFS is tracking a potential consolidation of 20–30 LBMC insurance verifiers. Accounting could scale from 9 to 30 under full centralization. Planning ranges with conservative floors and growth buffers are more reliable than point estimates.

06

An Anticipated RTO Policy Shift Is Creating Latent Demand

CHRO Mary Leahy anticipates the incoming CEO will increase hybrid work prevalence systemically, pulling employees currently classified as remote into hybrid — requiring more seats. The gap between the current survey count (100 hybrid) and the true or near-future hybrid number is potentially significant for seat supply planning. Other leaders (PFS, IS) indicate no expected behavioral change.
